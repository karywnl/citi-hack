## Correction to the supplied evidence

The friend's note described roughly 45% as an approximation based on separate male/female figures. **The official Table 51 actually publishes an “All” figure of 45.6%.** Use that published estimate and its correct denominator rather than calculating an average.

The narrative near Table 51 loosely says “1 in every 4” could not name a work option. For numerical claims use Table 50's exact **21.0%** and its response wording, rather than repeating this loose narrative approximation.

## Interpretation and limits

- These findings motivate investigating career exploration and exposure to people doing relevant work.
- They do not directly quantify the prevalence of every proposed problem, such as scholarship discovery or application barriers.
- Not having stated a work aspiration does not mean having no ambition, ability, or potential.
- Not knowing anyone doing the desired work is not identical to having no mentor or no access to a career counsellor.
- The data do not establish that a lack of guidance causes dropout or that our proposed product will improve employment.
- ASER's surveyed age range does not cover the full school-to-graduation range mentioned in the hackathon brief.
